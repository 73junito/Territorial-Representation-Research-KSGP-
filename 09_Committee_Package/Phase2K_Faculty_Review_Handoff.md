## Research scope

The project documents 2020 decennial age/sex and population data for five inhabited U.S. territories plus Washington, D.C., a federal district. Separately, it documents 32 *source-specific* socioeconomic observations across four domains: education, civilian unemployment, median household income, and poverty.

The 2020 Island Areas Census Demographic Profiles (AS/GU/MP/VI), 2019 one-year ACS/PRCS (D.C./PR), and 2016–2020 five-year ACS/PRCS (D.C./PR) differ in design, universes, inflation adjustment, and time reference. They are not one harmonized six-case analytical dataset.

## Essential questions for the reviewer

1. Do the research design and questions support the proposed **descriptive**, source-specific inferences and no stronger claims?
2. Are the four socioeconomic variables correctly matched to Census programs, age or household denominators, reference periods, and geographic exclusions?
3. Are the 12 published Census Data Profile percentage MOEs correctly identified, retrieved, and interpreted at the published precision?
4. Is the D.C. bachelor's-or-higher MOE diagnostic properly described? Phase 2F's quadrature of four count-category MOEs differs from the published Data Profile MOE; Phase 2I numerically reproduces the latter using the Census-published **composite-count** MOE. The precise covariance/replicate-variance structure was **not** independently reconstructed.
5. Are claims about Guam military-housing coverage and 2019 vs. 2020 inflation-adjusted income denominators adequately and accurately qualified?
6. Are the APA 7 bibliography entries, matching in-text references, statistical tables, and evidence-source links accurate and sufficient?
7. Is the committee brief suitable for *factual contextual review* without suggesting KSGP adoption, a political position, or causal/electoral inference?
8. Are the unresolved demographic SPSS `.sav` historical byte-hash discrepancies sufficiently disclosed, without implying data-content corruption?
