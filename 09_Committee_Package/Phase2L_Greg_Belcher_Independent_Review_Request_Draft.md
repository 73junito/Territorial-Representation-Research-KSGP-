Dear Dr. Belcher,

I hope you are doing well. I am writing to ask whether you would be willing to provide an independent statistical and methodological review of a research project I have been developing, *Territorial Representation Research*.

The study documents 2010–2020 demographic evidence and selected socioeconomic measures for Puerto Rico, Guam, American Samoa, the Northern Mariana Islands, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and Washington, D.C. It uses original U.S. Census Bureau sources, including the 2020 Island Areas Census Demographic Profiles and separate 2019 one-year and 2016–2020 five-year ACS/PRCS datasets.

Because I have benefited from your instruction in TTED 887, I would especially appreciate your evaluation of the statistical methods and interpretation limits. Twelve published 90% percentage margins of error have now been independently retrieved and cross-checked. In two D.C. education measures, a four-category count-MOE approximation differed from the Census-published percentage MOE; using the directly published composite count MOE reproduces the published percentage MOE at its reporting precision. I want to ensure that this methodology and its qualifications are stated correctly.

I would welcome your assessment of the denominator definitions, ACS/PRCS uncertainty procedures, Census product comparability, statistical limitations, and the academic presentation of the results. The project explicitly prohibits unqualified six-jurisdiction socioeconomic significance comparisons because the source products and population universes are not equivalent.
