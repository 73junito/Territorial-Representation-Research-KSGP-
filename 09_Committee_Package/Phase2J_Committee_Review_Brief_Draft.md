# Territorial Representation Research — Phase 2J Committee Review Brief

**Discussion draft only. No Kansas Green Party committee or organization endorsement or decision is implied.**

### Purpose
Summarize independently sourced Census demographics and cautiously describe socioeconomic evidence for five inhabited U.S. territories and Washington, D.C., a federal district. This is factual context, not a recommendation on voting representation or territorial political status.

### Evidence
- Six-jurisdiction 2020 Census population: **4,313,440**.
- Four domains: educational attainment, unemployment, median household income and poverty.
- **32 source-specific observations**, divided among 2020 Island Areas Census profiles (AS, GU, MP, VI), 2019 one-year ACS/PRCS (D.C., Puerto Rico), and 2016–2020 five-year ACS/PRCS (D.C., Puerto Rico).
- Archived official source tables, source URLs, checksums and methods are available in the research repository.

### Updated statistical acceptance
The Census Bureau's published Data Profile percentage MOEs were independently retrieved and verified for all twelve target measures. Ten Phase 2F derived MOEs already matched at one-decimal precision; two D.C. education differences were numerically reconciled using directly published **composite-count** MOEs. This improves the audit trail but **does not authorize pooled six-jurisdiction comparison**.

### Caution
Four Island Areas data sources are census long-form profiles, whereas Puerto Rico/D.C. figures are sample surveys. Universes, dollar years, reference periods and Guam coverage differ. Do not use the material for an unqualified six-way ranking, statistical significance conclusion, causal finding or inference about voting eligibility or political preferences.
